# TikTok research: what gets views, comments and shares (2026-11-21)

Overnight study requested by the owner: "look at everyone, not just streamers,
all clips in general — what gets the most views, most comments, most shares".
This adds TikTok evidence to the YouTube work in
[VIRAL_CONTENT_RESEARCH.md](VIRAL_CONTENT_RESEARCH.md).

- **Raw data:** `docs/research/tiktok_videos_2026-11-21.jsonl` (one public video per line)
- **All tables:** `docs/research/tiktok_analysis.md` (re-generated by the script)
- **Script:** `python -m src.clips.tiktok_research docs/research/tiktok_videos_2026-11-21.jsonl`
  (`src/clips/tiktok_research.py`, tests in `tests/test_tiktok_research.py`)

## 1. How the data was collected

- Claude browsed TikTok in the owner's Chrome, logged in to the owner's
  **personal** account (so the For You feed reflects the owner's taste).
  Read-only: nothing was liked, followed, commented, posted or changed.
- For every video the page showed, the public numbers TikTok already sends to
  the browser were saved: views, likes, comments, shares, saves, length,
  caption, hashtags, sound, posting time, and the creator's follower count.
  No video files were downloaded.
- Three sources:

| Source | Videos | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Owner's For You feed | 287 | What TikTok chooses to show the owner |
| 20 searches (funny, streamer clips, podcast clips, street interview, Kai Cenat, IShowSpeed, prank, comedy skit, fails, storytime, NBA, football skills, Fortnite, Roblox funny, GTA 6, Minecraft, movie clips, reaction, debate, satisfying) | 3,081 | A broad cross-section of genres |
| 41 creator profiles (~235 of each account's own videos) | 10,609 | Lets each account be compared **with itself** |

Total: **13,977 videos** (13,667 after removing 310 adverts) from **2,973 creators**.

### Why "compared with itself" matters

Across all videos, follower count explains most of the difference in views
(median views: 10K for accounts under 10K followers, 415K for 100K-1M).
That hides everything else. So the strongest evidence here (section 2) compares
each account's videos only with that same account's other videos, using
"views ÷ that account's usual (median) views". 1.00x means a normal video for
that account. A "breakout" means 2x or more. Videos younger than 7 days are
left out of that comparison because their views are still climbing.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 For streamer clips, longer clips win (the most useful result for us)

Twelve streamer-clip accounts (Kai Cenat, Speed, Jynxzi, N3on, StableRonaldo,
Lacy and others; ~1,900 settled videos). Each video is compared with the
**same account's videos posted within ±10 days**, so an account simply
growing over time cannot explain it:

| Length | Videos | x usual views | Breakouts (2x+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| under 10 s | 113 | 0.95x | 30% |
| 10-20 s | 468 | 0.92x | 31% |
| 20-30 s | 334 | 0.84x | 31% |
| 30-45 s | 248 | 0.97x | 32% |
| **45-60 s** | 119 | **1.43x** | 39% |
| **60-90 s** | 244 | **1.22x** | 39% |
| **90-180 s** | 76 | **1.61x** | 45% |
| 3 min+ | 20 | 2.11x | 50% (only 4 accounts) |

Six of the eight accounts with enough of both kinds show it on their own
(for example permheadtv: 60 s+ clips 1.53x vs under-30 s 0.85x; poggerclipz
1.85x vs 0.86x; jynxzi_clipz.z 1.98x vs 0.92x). Two did not (prestigeclipn,
baldnews_).

For all 36 profile accounts together (every genre) length makes little
difference (about 1.0x from 10 s to 3 min). So this is specific to streamer
clips: a longer clip has room for the setup, the moment and the reaction.

**This disagrees with our current settings** (reaction/gameplay clips 18-35 s).
It agrees with the owner's own notes on drafts 121-123 ("far too short", "missed
the best part at the end", "cut off mid-sentence").

### 2.2 Shares spread videos, comments do not

Within each account, how a video did compared with that account's usual
(rank correlation, 6,320 videos):

| Per 1,000 views | Correlation with "x usual views" |
|---|---|
| Shares | **+0.10** (more shares → bigger video) |
| Saves | +0.03 |
| Likes | +0.03 |
| Comments | **-0.12** (big videos get *fewer* comments per view) |

Each account's best quarter against its own worst quarter (36 accounts):
2.78 vs 1.96 shares per 1,000 views, but 0.55 vs 0.79 comments. Hits reach
people who are not fans; those people share but rarely comment.

### 2.3 What each number rewards (from the top-15 lists)

- **Most views:** the streamers' own accounts (IShowSpeed 264M, Kai Cenat 137M)
  with one-hashtag captions. Next come curiosity explainers ("What happens if
  you wear a cap every day?", ~60 s, a 5.3M-follower Spanish account had 7 of
  the top 15). Then quick fails.
- **Most shares:** very short (5-15 s), wordless, relatable fails, memes and
  animal clips, often from **tiny accounts** (a 6K-follower account: 45M views
  and 2.7M shares from a 5-second clip). People share what a friend will get
  instantly.
- **Most comments:** the streamers' own accounts and opinion/debate content.
  The highest comment *rates* (20+ per 1,000 views) were an account posting
  political takes and a "teach me" series. Questions people want to answer
  draw comments.
- **Most saves:** dances, football skills, game tips. Things people want to
  rewatch or copy.
- **Breakout reach** (views far above followers): short fails, "wait for it"
  moments and memes from accounts with under 1K followers.

By length, across all videos with 10K+ views: shares per view are highest at
20-45 s and lowest at 3 min+; comments and saves per view rise with length.

### 2.4 Captions barely matter (same result as YouTube)

Compared with each account's own usual views, almost every caption feature
was close to 1.00x:

| Caption feature | With | Without |
|---|---|---|
| Asks a question | 1.01x | 1.00x |
| Any emoji | 1.01x | 0.97x |
| 😭 | 0.92x | 1.01x |
| A SHOUTED word ("Kai Cenat got EXCITED") | 1.04x | 1.00x |
| @mention | 1.02x | 1.00x |
| Original sound (not a song) | 1.00x | 0.96x |
| Caption length 6-12 words | 1.04x | 0.91x-0.99x for others |

For streamer clips alone, a SHOUTED word did a bit better (1.18x vs 0.97x,
breakouts 42% vs 32%). That is a small, possibly style-of-account effect.
Posting time (4-hour blocks, US Central) ranged only 0.92x-1.03x.

**Hashtags:** between accounts, 6+ hashtags looked terrible (4% hits), but
that is because spam-like tiny accounts use many hashtags. Within an account
the count made no consistent difference. 3-5 relevant hashtags is fine. The
5-hashtag cap we already use is right.

The YouTube study (section 9 of VIRAL_CONTENT_RESEARCH.md) found the same
thing: within a channel, hits and flops have the same title and caption
style. **The moment is what matters.**

### 2.5 The owner's For You feed

- 89% of the videos TikTok showed the owner came from accounts with **under
  100K followers** (median 6.5K followers, median 338K views). TikTok readily
  pushes small accounts, which is good news for a new channel.
- Median length 43 s.
- Many were streamer/podcast clips, street interviews, skits and GTA 6.

### 2.6 GTA 6 right now

GTA 6 is the topic of the week. The fastest videos seen were 1-day-old GTA 6
clips with 14-22M views. But it is crowded: among recent videos (under 7 days,
10K+ follower accounts), GTA 6 videos had a *lower* median (6K views/day,
1,538 videos) than other videos (12K/day, 1,723 videos). A GTA clip needs a
real moment. The topic alone does not carry it, which matches the YouTube
finding that "in GTA" titles were common among flops.

## 3. What this suggests for ShortsEngine (for the owner to decide)

Nothing was changed in the pipeline; these are proposals.

1. **Allow longer streamer clips.** Let the editor keep 45-90 s (up to ~2 min)
   when the moment has a build-up and a payoff, instead of 18-35 s. For
   example: reaction/gameplay 25-90 s, awkward/story 30-120 s. Keep short cuts
   only for a single instant moment (one jump-scare, one line).
2. **Rank moments by "would someone send this to a friend?"** Shares are the
   number that goes with bigger reach. Comments do not. The editor prompt could
   ask for shareability (an instantly understood, surprising or relatable
   moment) rather than "controversial / ask a question".
3. **Keep captions simple.** Name + what happens, 6-12 words, 3-5 specific
   hashtags. Caption tricks (questions, emoji, capitals) showed no reliable
   effect, so the packaging experiments should not expect big wins from them.
4. **GTA 6:** clip it only when something actually happens. The topic is
   saturated.

## 4. Limits (read before trusting the numbers)

- **Search results and profiles are not random.** Search shows popular videos
  first. Profiles show the latest ~235 videos of accounts that were chosen
  *because* they appeared in searches.
- **The For You sample is small** (287 videos) and is one person's feed.
- **Views are cumulative.** Age is controlled for by leaving out videos under
  7 days old and, in 2.1, by comparing within ±10 days. Older videos may have
  had a different algorithm.
- **Correlation, not cause.** Longer streamer clips may do better partly
  because accounts save longer cuts for better moments. The ±10-day check
  removes account growth, not that. A real test is to post both lengths of
  similar moments: the packaging experiment in `src/clips/packaging.py`
  could add clip length as a dimension.
- **No watch time or retention.** TikTok only shows those to the account
  owner. Our own TikTok analytics would add it.
- Only public numbers that TikTok shows on its pages were saved. Captions are
  stored as written. No private data about the owner's account was saved
  (the owner's own likes/saves on each video were deliberately left out).
